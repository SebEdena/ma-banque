# Spec — Statistics

**Status:** ready-for-agent

Complements [00-business-requirements.md](./00-business-requirements.md) and [technical-architecture.md](../architecture/technical-architecture.md). Blocked by [06-entries.md](./06-entries.md) (the `entries` table's `date`/`amount`/`category_id` columns and the `EntryRepository` surface it finalized) and [04-categories.md](./04-categories.md) (category `name`/`color`/`icon`, for the breakdown's display), both written and implemented per [dependencies.md](./dependencies.md). This is a read-only, aggregate-only feature: **no new entity, no new table, no migration.** Note that this spec **corrects business requirements §4's sidebar wording** on where the statistics screen is reached from — the same kind of prototype-driven correction `04-categories.md` already made when it moved "Postes" out of the sidebar rail and into Settings; see the Solution section.

## Problem Statement

An account's entries screen (`06-entries.md`) shows activity one row at a time, and nothing in the app answers the two questions business requirements §4.5 asks: where does the money actually go (which _Postes_ absorb the spending), and how do _Recettes_ and _Dépenses_ compare month over month. A user with a year of entries can scroll their register indefinitely without ever seeing either shape. Business requirements §8 flagged this screen as the one area never checked against the prototype, so its entry point, its period control, and its two visualizations were all unsettled until now.

## Solution

Build the **Statistiques** screen (business requirements §4.5): scoped to one account at a time, over a selectable period, showing a breakdown of expenses by _Poste_ and a month-by-month _Recettes_/_Dépenses_ comparison. Both aggregates are computed in SQL by two new `EntryRepository` methods and served through a new `usecases::statistics` module and a `commands::statistics` Tauri surface; the frontend renders them with [Unovis](https://unovis.dev) (`@unovis/ts` + `@unovis/angular`), the app's first charting dependency.

**Cross-checked against the existing prototype** (`docs/design/design.html`, the `isStats` screen), settling everything business requirements §8 left open. Four things it settles, one of them differently than a literal reading of §4:

- **The statistics screen is reached from the account header, not the sidebar rail.** Business requirements §4 lists "statistics" among the things the persistent sidebar gives access to. The prototype instead puts a **"Statistiques" button (bar-chart icon) in the account header**, alongside "Pointage" and "Paramètres", and the statistics screen carries a **"Voir le compte" button (wallet icon)** back to the account it is showing. There is no statistics icon in the main icon rail. This spec follows the prototype: statistics is an account-scoped screen reached from an account, exactly as `04-categories.md` concluded that "Postes" is a Settings sub-section rather than the top-level sidebar screen §4's sentence implies. Business requirements §4's sidebar sentence is treated as superseded on this point, not as an unbuilt requirement.
- **The period control is four fixed presets — "1 mois", "3 mois", "6 mois", "12 mois"** — rendered as a segmented control, not a free from/to date picker. "Selectable time range" in §4.5 means choosing among these, and the range is always the N months ending today.
- **The account scope is switched from within the screen**, via a row of account pills (colour dot + name, the active one visually selected) below the header. The screen still shows exactly one account's data at a time — this is a switcher, not a consolidated view, and the "no consolidated cross-account view" line in §4.5 and §7 stands unchanged.
- **The two visualizations are a donut and a grouped bar chart**, described in detail under Implementation Decisions. Both are drawn by hand in the prototype (SVG `<path>` segments; plain `div`s with pixel heights); this spec builds them with Unovis's `Donut` and `GroupedBar` components instead of reproducing that hand-rolled markup, since both chart shapes map directly onto components the library already provides.

## User Stories

1. As a user, I want a "Statistiques" button in the account header next to "Pointage" and "Paramètres", so that I can jump from reading an account's register to seeing its shape without hunting through navigation.
2. As a user, I want the statistics screen to open already showing the account I was just looking at, so that clicking through feels like a change of view rather than a change of subject.
3. As a user, I want a "Voir le compte" button on the statistics screen, so that I can return to the register of whichever account I am currently analysing.
4. As a user, I want a row of account pills on the statistics screen, so that I can compare one account's picture against another's without going back to the home screen in between.
5. As a user, I want each account pill to carry that account's colour, so that I can recognise the account I want at a glance the same way I do on the home screen and in the sidebar.
6. As a user, I want to choose a period of 1, 3, 6 or 12 months, so that I can look at recent behaviour or a longer trend depending on what I am trying to understand.
7. As a user, I want the period I chose to stay chosen when I switch accounts, so that I am comparing like with like rather than silently resetting to a different window.
8. As a user, I want a donut chart breaking my expenses down by _Poste_ over the chosen period, so that I can see which categories absorb my spending rather than reading a list of numbers.
9. As a user, I want each donut slice coloured with its _Poste_'s own colour, so that the chart and the rest of the app agree about what each category looks like.
10. As a user, I want the total expenses for the period displayed in the middle of the donut, so that I have the absolute figure alongside the proportions.
11. As a user, I want a legend listing each _Poste_ with its colour, name, percentage and amount, so that I can read exact figures the chart itself can only approximate.
12. As a user, I want entries with no _Poste_ assigned gathered into an explicit "Sans poste" slice rather than dropped, so that the donut's total always matches my real spending for the period and I can see how much is still unclassified.
13. As a user, I want a clear "Aucune dépense sur cette période." message when the chosen period contains no expenses, so that an empty chart never reads as a loading failure.
14. As a user, I want a chart comparing _Recettes_ and _Dépenses_ for each month in the period, so that I can see whether I am living within my means month to month.
15. As a user, I want the two bars per month to use the app's existing positive/negative colours with a legend naming them, so that income and expense are distinguishable without me having to guess which bar is which.
16. As a user, I want every month in the chosen period to appear on the chart even if it had no activity, so that a gap in my records reads as a gap rather than as a shorter period.
17. As a user, I want to hover a bar and see its exact amount, so that I can get the precise figure without a separate table (via Unovis's built-in tooltip, not a hand-wired hover handler).
18. As a user, I want the opening-balance system entry left out of both charts, so that the month I opened the account isn't dominated by a figure that isn't a real transaction.
19. As a user, I want every amount on this screen formatted with my configured currency and date preferences, so that statistics reads the same way as the rest of the app.
20. As a user, I want the current account's accent colour applied to the screen's interactive elements, so that I always know which account I am looking at, consistent with the entries screen.
21. As a user, I want the statistics screen to stay responsive on an account with years of history, so that opening it never feels like a penalty for having used the app for a long time.
22. As a developer, I want the two aggregations computed in SQL rather than by fetching entries and summing them in application code, so that the volume crossing the repository boundary stays proportional to what is displayed rather than to how long the account has existed.
23. As a developer, I want the aggregate methods added to `EntryRepository` rather than to a new repository, so that the entries data-access surface stays in one place as `06-entries.md` intended.
24. As a developer, I want the percentages and per-month totals computed in Rust and returned ready to display, so that the "Rust owns all calculations" rule holds and Angular is never a second, unsynchronized site for arithmetic on money.
25. As a developer, I want a `StatisticsApi` service on the frontend mirroring `EntriesApi`/`CategoriesApi`, so that the statistics components are testable against a mocked API rather than against `invoke()`.
26. As a developer, I want the two charts built with Unovis's `Donut` and `GroupedBar` components rather than hand-rolled SVG/CSS, so that arc geometry, bar layout, and hover tooltips come from a maintained library instead of being reimplemented and re-tested by hand for a single screen.

## Implementation Decisions

- **No schema change.** This feature reads `entries` (`date`, `amount`, `category_id`, `is_system`, `account_id`) and `categories` (`name`, `color`, `icon`) exactly as `06-entries.md` and `04-categories.md` left them. No migration, no new column, no index change is anticipated; the existing `entries_account_date` index already covers the account+date range scan both aggregates perform. If profiling during implementation shows a need for a `category_id` index, adding one is a pure additive migration and the implementer's call.
- **Aggregation happens in SQL, in the repository.** Two new methods on `EntryRepository` (extended, not replaced — same treatment `06-entries.md` gave it), each a single `GROUP BY` returning one row per bucket rather than one row per entry:
  - a **category-breakdown aggregate** over an account and an inclusive date range, returning per-category summed expense totals joined to the category's `name`/`color`/`icon`, with uncategorised rows collapsed into a single null-category bucket;
  - a **month-bucketed aggregate** over the same account and range, returning per-month summed income and summed expense totals.

  This is the decisive point of the spec and it follows the precedent already on the trait: `sum_by_account` and `count_by_category` are aggregate SQL methods, not "fetch rows and count in Rust". Fetching raw entries to sum them in a pure domain function would be unit-testable without a database, but it would move the entire history of an account across the repository boundary to produce twelve numbers — directly against the "years of history" concern `06-entries.md` built its whole pagination contract around. The aggregation is trivially expressible in SQL and non-trivially expensive to do anywhere else.

- **Sign convention and the system entry.** Amounts are `SignedCents` per `technical-architecture.md` §1.3: expenses are negative, income positive. The category breakdown covers **expenses only** (matching the prototype's "Répartition des dépenses par poste" title) and reports each bucket's total as a positive magnitude. The monthly aggregate splits each month into an income total and an expense total, both reported as positive magnitudes so the frontend sizes two bars without re-deriving a sign. **Both aggregates exclude the system entry** (`is_system = 1`) in their `WHERE` clauses: the opening balance is not a period transaction, carries no category, and would otherwise dominate whichever month the account was opened in. This matches how `03-accounts.md` and `06-entries.md` already scope the system entry out of `count_non_system_by_account` and `exists_non_system_on_or_before`.
- **Uncategorised entries.** The null-`category_id` bucket is surfaced as a real slice labelled **"Sans poste"**, not dropped — `06-entries.md` deliberately allows an entry to have no category (its user story 9), so omitting the bucket would make the donut's total silently disagree with the period's actual spending. It carries a neutral colour (the app's muted/border token, not a category colour) and no icon, and it sorts last regardless of size so it reads as a residual rather than as a competing category.
- **Percentages and totals are computed in Rust.** The response for the category breakdown carries, per bucket, the display name, colour, icon, the amount, **and its percentage of the period's expense total**; the response also carries the period's expense total itself. Per `technical-architecture.md` §1.3, Angular never divides one money figure by another. Angular's remaining job is purely geometric: turning a percentage into an SVG arc's `d` attribute, and turning an amount into a bar's pixel height relative to the largest value on the chart. That geometry is presentation, not a calculation on money, and stays on the frontend.
- **Month bucketing and gap filling.** The SQL aggregate returns only months that had activity; the use case **fills in the missing months with zeroes** so the returned series always covers every month of the requested period in chronological order. Doing this in the use case rather than the query keeps the SQL simple and makes gap filling a behaviour the fake-repository tests can cover directly. Month labels are produced from the month key by the frontend, per the display-format settings from `05-settings-remainder.md`.
- **Period presets resolve to a date range in Rust.** The command accepts the period as one of four preset values (1/3/6/12 months), not as a raw from/to pair; `usecases::statistics` derives the inclusive `IsoDate` range — the N-month window ending today — and passes it to the repository. Keeping the presets in the domain means the frontend never does date arithmetic, and the "what does '3 mois' mean exactly" question has one answer in one place. The range boundaries are inclusive at both ends, consistent with `06-entries.md`'s `EntryListQuery`.
- **Module layout.** `usecases::statistics` (one use case per chart, or one returning both — implementer's call, as long as the period→range derivation is shared rather than duplicated) and `commands::statistics`, mirroring the `Account`/`Category`/`Entry` module shape. A `domain::statistics` module holds the response types and the period enum. **No new repository trait**: the two aggregate methods go on `EntryRepository`, which is where entries data access lives. No new error enum either — the aggregates can fail only on I/O or an invalid stored value, both of which `EntryError` already models; reusing it avoids a near-duplicate enum for a read-only feature that has no business rules to reject.
- **Frontend.** A `StatisticsApi` injectable service (`src/app/data/statistics`, mirroring `EntriesApi`/`CategoriesApi`) wraps `invoke()` for the new commands. A new routed `Statistics` screen renders: the "Statistiques" title, the "Voir le compte" button, the four-preset segmented period control, the account pill row (fed by `AccountsApi.listAccounts()` — no new backend command), and the two cards. Changing the period or the selected account re-requests both aggregates. The account header on the entries screen (`06-entries.md`'s `Account` component) gains the "Statistiques" button that routes here with the current account preselected.
- **The donut** is Unovis's `Donut` component (`vis-donut` in the `VisDonutModule`), one segment per bucket, `colorAccessor` returning the bucket's colour (the "Sans poste" bucket's neutral colour included), `centralLabel`/`centralSubLabel` carrying the formatted total and the "Total dépenses" caption. Unovis renders the arcs and owns their geometry; this spec supplies data and colour only. The legend — colour swatch, name, percentage, right-aligned monospace amount — is not a Unovis feature and stays hand-built, since the library has no built-in legend and the row shape here (percentage _and_ amount, not just a label) is specific to this screen. The empty state replaces the whole chart+legend block with "Aucune dépense sur cette période."
- **The monthly chart** is Unovis's `GroupedBar` inside an `XYContainer` (`vis-xy-container` / `vis-grouped-bar` / `vis-axis`), two y-accessors per month (income, expense) coloured with the existing `--positive`/`--negative` tokens via `colorAccessor`, month labels on the x-axis. Hover tooltips use Unovis's built-in `Tooltip`/`TooltipModule` rather than a hand-wired `title` attribute. A legend above the chart names the two colours "Recettes" and "Dépenses" — hand-built for the same reason as the donut's.
- **Unovis is a new dependency**: `@unovis/ts` (core) and `@unovis/angular` (the Angular component wrappers), Apache-2.0 licensed. This is a decision, not an open question — Unovis's `Donut` and `GroupedBar` map directly onto the two shapes this screen needs, sparing a hand-rolled reimplementation of arc geometry, bar layout, and hover interaction for a single screen. Before implementation, verify `@unovis/angular`'s currently-supported Angular version range against this project's `^22.1.0` (Unovis 1.7+ tracks Angular's own LTS window rather than a fixed version floor, per its release notes) and pin accordingly.
- **Amount and date formatting** goes through `DisplaySettingsService`'s existing `currency-format`/`date-format` pipes (`05-settings-remainder.md`) exactly as the entries and home screens already do — this spec introduces no formatting logic.
- **Accent colour.** The statistics screen is scoped to one account, so business requirements §5's rule applies in full: the selected account's colour drives the screen's interactive elements (the active period preset, the active account pill, button hovers). §5's parenthetical excusing "statistics if not scoped to one account" does not apply, since the prototype settles that this screen always is.
- **Error display.** A failed aggregate surfaces as a toast per `technical-architecture.md` §2.2. There is no field-level validation on this screen — it has no inputs, only selectors.

## Testing Decisions

- SQLite integration tests for the **category-breakdown aggregate** against a fresh `:memory:` database with all migrations applied: entries across several categories sum per category; income entries are excluded so the result is expenses only; entries with `category_id IS NULL` collapse into exactly one uncategorised bucket; the system entry never appears in any bucket; entries falling exactly on the range's `from` and `to` dates are both included and entries one day outside either end are both excluded; another account's entries never leak into the result.
- SQLite integration tests for the **month-bucketed aggregate** against the same setup: entries in the same calendar month sum together and entries in adjacent months do not; income and expense totals are reported separately and both as positive magnitudes; a month whose entries are all income reports a zero expense total (and vice versa); the system entry is excluded; range boundaries are inclusive at both ends; another account's entries never leak in.
- Use-case tests against a hand-written in-memory fake `EntryRepository` (no `mockall`, per `technical-architecture.md` §1.6) covering only what the use case itself decides: each of the four presets derives the expected inclusive date range ending today; months with no activity are filled in as zero-valued entries in the series; the returned series is chronologically ordered and covers every month of the period; percentages are computed against the period's expense total and a period with no expenses yields an empty breakdown and a zero total rather than a division error; the uncategorised bucket is labelled "Sans poste" and sorted last even when it is the largest.
- No pure geometry unit test for the donut arcs: Unovis owns arc-path computation internally, so there is nothing left in this spec's own code to test at that level — testing it would mean testing the library, not this feature. The percentage arithmetic feeding `Donut`'s data is already covered by the use-case tests above.
- Angular component tests (Vitest) for `Statistics` against a **mocked `StatisticsApi`** (never `invoke()` directly, per the seam established from `03-accounts.md` onward): the screen requests both aggregates for the preselected account on load; choosing a different period preset re-requests both with the new period; selecting a different account pill re-requests both for that account while keeping the chosen period; the `Donut` component receives one data point per returned bucket with the expected colour/value, and the hand-built legend renders one row per bucket with the returned colours, names, percentages and amounts; the "Aucune dépense sur cette période." empty state renders when the breakdown comes back empty; the `GroupedBar` component receives one data point per returned month (including zero-valued ones) with both series populated; a failing API call surfaces a toast. Assertions are on the inputs bound into the Unovis components (their `data`/`color`/`x`/`y` bindings) and on the hand-built legend's rendered rows, never on Unovis's internal SVG output.
- Angular component test for the entries screen's new entry point: the account header's "Statistiques" button navigates to the statistics screen with the current account preselected.
- E2E (WebdriverIO, `e2e/statistics.e2e.ts`, sharing the WDIO session per `technical-architecture.md` §2.3): from an account with entries created by the test setup, open the statistics screen via the account header button, assert both charts render with data, switch the period preset and assert the charts update, switch to another account via the pills, and return to the register via "Voir le compte" — asserting on `data-testid` hooks.

## Out of Scope

- **A consolidated cross-account statistics view** — explicitly out of scope for v1 per business requirements §4.5 and §7. The account pills switch which single account is charted; they never aggregate across accounts.
- **A free from/to date-range picker.** The period is one of the four presets, per the prototype. A custom range is a plausible later evolution and nothing here precludes it, since the repository methods already take an arbitrary inclusive range.
- **Income breakdown by category.** The donut is expenses only, per the prototype's title and business requirements §4.5's wording.
- **Trend lines, forecasts, budgets, or category targets** — nothing in business requirements asks for them.
- **Exporting or printing statistics** — not in v1.
- **Reconciliation state as a statistics dimension** (`08-reconciliation.md` owns the `reconciled` flag's aggregate uses); the charts count every entry in the period regardless of whether it has been pointed.
- **Recurring-entry projections** (`07-recurring-entries.md`); statistics only ever reflects entries that already exist.
- **Any sidebar rail change.** This spec adds no icon to the main rail — see the entry-point correction above.

## Further Notes

- This is the last of the three layer-3 specs per `dependencies.md`, and the only one that is purely read-only. It adds no entity, no table, and no business rule that can be violated, which is why it reuses `EntryError` rather than introducing a `StatisticsError` with nothing to put in it.
- It is genuinely independent of `07-recurring-entries.md` and `08-reconciliation.md` — it touches no column either of them modifies — so it can be built concurrently with both. Its only conflict surface is `EntryRepository`, which it extends additively.
- `dependencies.md` should be updated when this lands, moving `09-statistics` out of "not yet written".
- **Unovis version check.** `@unovis/angular` 1.7+ supports only Angular versions currently in their LTS window rather than a broad fixed range; confirm the version installed supports `^22.1.0` before adding the dependency, and re-check on any future Angular upgrade in this repo, since Unovis has stated it will drop a version's support as that version leaves Angular's own LTS window.
- The business-requirements-vs-prototype correction on the entry point is worth carrying back: business requirements §4's sidebar sentence now has two known exceptions (`04-categories.md`'s "Postes" and this spec's "Statistiques"), which is enough of a pattern that the sidebar description should probably be reworded to just "every account, plus settings" rather than accumulating a third footnote.
- If a later evolution does want a consolidated view, the month-bucketed aggregate is the one that generalises cleanly (drop the account predicate, or take a list of account ids); the category breakdown would need a decision about whether per-account colours still mean anything once accounts are merged.
