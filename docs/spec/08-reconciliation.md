# Spec — Reconciliation

**Status:** ready-for-agent

Complements [00-business-requirements.md](./00-business-requirements.md) (§3.5 and §4.3's reconciliation-panel bullet) and [technical-architecture.md](../architecture/technical-architecture.md). Blocked by [06-entries.md](./06-entries.md), which built the per-entry `reconciled` boolean and its row checkbox and explicitly deferred the panel, the aggregate calculation, and the "unreconciled only" filter to this spec — per [dependencies.md](./dependencies.md), `06` is written and implemented, so this one is unblocked. Independent of `07-recurring-entries.md` and `09-statistics.md`: all three sit in layer 3 and touch nothing of each other's.

**This spec adds nothing to the home screen.** `dependencies.md`'s "Settled since the graph was first drawn" records "no reconciliation status indicator on the home screen, ever (`03-accounts.md`) — not deferred to `08-reconciliation`". Business requirements §4.1 literally asks for a red/green indicator per account card; `03-accounts.md` decided against it during spec review, matching the prototype, and that decision is not reopened here. The panel built by this spec is the **only** place reconciliation status ever appears. Anyone reading §4.1 and concluding this spec left a gap should read this paragraph instead: it is a settled cut, not an oversight.

## Problem Statement

Every entry already carries a `reconciled` (_pointée_) boolean and a checkbox to toggle it from its row, but nothing reads that flag back. A user can tick off the transactions that appear on their bank statement and get no answer to the only question the ticking is for: **does my register agree with the bank?** There is no reconciled balance, no place to record what the bank actually says the balance is, no statement cut-off date to reconcile against, and no way to narrow the list down to the entries still waiting to be ticked. The flag is write-only, and the reconciliation workflow business requirements §3.5 describes does not exist.

## Solution

Build reconciliation (_Pointage_) end to end as business requirements §3.5 and §4.3 define it: two new per-account stored values (`bank_balance`, `statement_date`), a `reconciled_balance` aggregate over the account's ticked entries, the `delta` between the two, and the collapsible panel on the account/entries screen that displays all four with a red/green verdict and gates the "unreconciled only" list filter.

There is **no new entity**. Business requirements §3.5 opens with "no dedicated stored entity beyond the `reconciled` field on each entry, plus two settings values per account", and this spec holds to that: two nullable columns on `accounts`, and everything else derived on read.

**Cross-checked against the existing prototype** (`docs/design/design.html`), which settles three things a literal reading of §4.3 leaves open, and where this spec deliberately diverges from it on a fourth:

- The panel is a horizontal strip above the date-range filter row, reading _Solde pointé_ / _Solde relevé bancaire_ (an inline dashed-underline input, not a boxed form field) / _Écart_, separated by thin vertical rules, with a checkbox pushed to the far right of the same strip and a pill badge carrying the verdict (`Comptes pointés` on `--pointage-ok-soft`, `Écart détecté` on `--pointage-bad-soft`). It is opened by a "Pointage" toggle button sitting next to the existing "Paramètres" button in the account header. The prototype's checkbox reads "Non pointées uniquement", ticked to exclude reconciled entries; this spec's checkbox is the inverse framing, "Inclure lignes pointées", ticked (the default) to keep them included — same strip, same position, same resulting list when unticked, opposite polarity and label.
- The `--pointage-ok` / `--pointage-ok-soft` / `--pointage-bad` / `--pointage-bad-soft` tokens `06-entries.md` deferred are defined here, as the theme variables the prototype already declares in both its light and dark palettes. `06`'s row checkbox keeps the plain checked/unchecked style it shipped with; this spec does not retint it.
- The prototype counts the opening-balance system entry in its reconciled total unconditionally (`selPointedTotal = opening + reconciled entries`). This spec does the same, and Implementation Decisions explains why that isn't a contradiction of §3.5's formula.
- **Divergence:** the prototype has no statement-date control at all in the panel, and computes its delta the other way round (`reconciled_balance − bank_balance`) and displays it through `Math.abs()`. Business requirements §3.5 and §4.3 are explicit and unambiguous on both points — statement date is editable, and `delta = bank_balance − reconciled_balance` — so the requirements win. The prototype's omission reads as an oversight in a mock-up whose fixture data happened to be reconciled, not a considered cut; unlike the home-screen indicator above, nothing anywhere records it as a decision.

## User Stories

1. As a user, I want a "Pointage" button on my account's screen that opens a reconciliation panel, so that reconciling is one click away from the register I'm already looking at.
2. As a user, I want that panel collapsed by default, so that the screen stays about my entries on the many visits where I'm not reconciling.
3. As a user, I want the panel to stay collapsed when I open the account, never remembering that I left it open last time, so that every visit to an account starts from the same predictable place.
4. As a user, I want the panel to show my reconciled balance — the total of the entries I've ticked, up to my statement's cut-off date — so that I can see what my register says the bank should be holding.
5. As a user, I want to type in the balance my bank statement actually shows, so that the app has both numbers it needs to tell me whether they agree.
6. As a user, I want the bank balance I type to be saved against the account and still be there next time I open the panel, so that I don't retype it every session.
7. As a user, I want to set the statement's cut-off date, so that entries I've made since my statement was issued don't get counted against a balance that predates them.
8. As a user, I want to change that cut-off date whenever a new statement arrives, so that the panel follows my statements month after month instead of being a one-time setup.
9. As a user, I want the difference between the two balances shown as a single "Écart" figure, so that I know at a glance how far off I am rather than doing the subtraction myself.
10. As a user, I want the difference shown in green when it's exactly zero and red otherwise, so that "everything agrees" is a state I can recognize without reading a number.
11. As a user, I want the difference shown with its sign, so that I know whether the bank thinks I have more money than my register does or less, which is what tells me whether I'm missing an entry or have a duplicate.
12. As a user, I want a short verdict label next to the figures ("Comptes pointés" / "Écart détecté"), so that the state is legible to me even before I've learned what the colors mean.
13. As a user, I want the panel to prompt me to set a statement date rather than showing a made-up balance, when I haven't set one yet, so that I'm never shown a confident-looking number the app actually had to guess at.
14. As a user, I want an "Inclure lignes pointées" checkbox in the panel that, unticked, hides the entries I've already ticked, so that reconciling becomes a shrinking to-do list instead of a hunt through everything.
15. As a user, I want that checkbox already ticked when I open the panel, so that already-reconciled entries stay visible by default and I have to opt into narrowing the list rather than opt out of it.
16. As a user, I want to untick it while the panel is open and see only the entries I still have to reconcile, so that I can focus on what's left without closing the panel and losing my place.
17. As a user, I want the checkbox grayed out and unusable when every entry in the account is already reconciled, so that I'm not offered a filter that would empty the list for no reason.
18. As a user, I want collapsing the panel to restore the full list immediately, whatever state I left the checkbox in, so that the filter can never be left on invisibly and leave me wondering where my entries went.
19. As a user, I want an entry to disappear from the list the moment I tick it while the filter is on, so that the list reflects the filter I asked for rather than going stale under my hands.
20. As a user, I want the reconciled balance and difference to update as soon as I tick or untick an entry, so that I can watch the gap close as I work through my statement.
21. As a user, I want ticking an entry dated after my statement date to leave the reconciled balance alone, so that the figure keeps meaning "as of my statement" rather than drifting as I get ahead of the bank.
22. As a user, I want my account's opening balance counted in the reconciled balance, so that the figure is comparable to a real bank balance instead of being short by the amount the account started with.
23. As a user, I want the opening-balance row to stay visible while the "unreconciled only" filter is on, so that the register keeps its anchor even in a filtered view.
24. As a user, I want the panel's controls tinted with my account's accent color like everything else on this screen, so that it doesn't read as a bolted-on section.
25. As a user, I want the reconciliation figures formatted with the same currency settings as every other amount in the app, so that nothing on this screen is formatted differently from the rest.
26. As a user, I want a bank balance I type that isn't a valid amount rejected next to the field, so that I can fix the typo without losing what I typed.
27. As a user, I want each of my accounts to keep its own bank balance and statement date, so that reconciling one account never disturbs another.
28. As a user, I want the panel to keep working on an account with years of entries without a pause, so that reconciling doesn't get slower the longer I use the app.
29. As a developer, I want the reconciled-balance total computed by a SQL aggregate rather than by summing a fetched list, so that the figure never depends on how much of the register the frontend happens to have paged in.
30. As a developer, I want `bank_balance` and `statement_date` reachable through their own narrow repository trait rather than bolted onto `AccountRepository`, so that the account settings modal's create/edit shape stays untouched by a feature it doesn't present.
31. As a developer, I want the difference and the green/red verdict expressed as pure functions over two integers, so that the rule at the heart of the feature is verifiable without a database or a repository.
32. As a developer, I want "unreconciled only" to be a real backend query parameter, so that a page of results is a page of matching results and pagination keeps meaning what it means.
33. As a developer, I want the count of unreconciled entries returned by the same call that returns the balances, so that deciding whether to gray the checkbox costs no extra round trip.
34. As a developer, I want reconciliation errors as a `thiserror` enum serialized to Angular like every other error surface in the app, so that the panel can show a precise message per failure case.

## Implementation Decisions

### Schema

- New migration `migrations/0007_accounts_reconciliation.sql` adds two **nullable** columns to `accounts`: `bank_balance INTEGER` (cents, `i64`, never a `REAL` — `technical-architecture.md` §1.3) and `statement_date TEXT` (ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`, matching every other date column).
- Both default to `NULL` for existing accounts **and** for newly created ones. `create_account` is not changed to seed them. `NULL` is a real, meaningful, first-class state here — "the user has not told us this yet" — not a placeholder to be papered over:
  - A `NULL` `statement_date` means **`reconciled_balance` is not computed at all**. There is no defensible default: today's date, the last entry's date, and the account's opening date are three different answers with three different meanings, and picking one would show the user a confident-looking figure the app in fact guessed. The panel shows a prompt to set a statement date instead of a number.
  - A `NULL` `bank_balance` means the delta is not computed either, and the field renders empty rather than pre-filled. Seeding it from the opening balance (as the prototype's fixtures do) would assert that the bank agrees with the register, which is precisely the claim the feature exists to test.
  - Neither column is ever written back to `NULL` by the UI once set; clearing is not an offered action. (An implementer must still handle `NULL` on read forever, since every account that predates this migration starts there.)
- The `entries` table is **not** touched. `06-entries.md`'s "Further Notes" anticipated this correctly: this spec reads the existing `reconciled`, `date`, and `amount` columns and adds only aggregate queries over them.

### Storage and repository surface

- New `domain::reconciliation` module, with a narrow `ReconciliationRepository` trait covering exactly the two stored values: reading them for an account, and setting each one. Concrete `infra::reconciliation` implementation holding the shared `Arc<Mutex<Connection>>` like every other repository (`technical-architecture.md` §1.5). `infra::entry` already coexists with `infra::account` over overlapping tables, so a second module reading `accounts` is an established shape, not a new one.
- **Deliberately not on `AccountRepository`.** That trait's `create`/`update` are contractually atomic with the account's system-entry write and are driven by `AccountDetails`, the single shared shape behind the create/edit settings modal (`03-accounts.md`). Adding the two reconciliation fields there would push them into a modal that must not show them (§4.3 puts the editing UI in the panel, not §4.2's settings screen) and would entangle a per-field panel edit with the opening-date/opening-balance transaction. The narrow trait keeps the modal's contract untouched.
- Two new methods on `EntryRepository` — extended, not reworked, so `06-entries.md`'s freshly-settled surface stays stable:
  - **`sum_reconciled_up_to(account_id, statement_date) -> i64`**: signed sum in cents of the account's entries where `reconciled = 1` **or** `is_system = 1`, and `date <= statement_date`. A direct sibling of the existing `sum_by_account`, differing only in its `WHERE` clause.
  - **`count_unreconciled_by_account(account_id) -> i64`**: how many non-system entries have `reconciled = 0`. Drives the checkbox's disabled state; a dedicated `COUNT(*)`, grouped with the two counting methods already on the trait.
- **The system entry counts as reconciled.** `06-entries.md` made `set_reconciled` reject the system entry, so the opening balance can never be ticked — meaning a literal reading of §3.5's "entries where `reconciled = true`" would permanently exclude it and leave `delta` off by the opening balance forever, making the green state unreachable for any account that didn't open at zero. That is plainly not what §3.5 intends: a bank's balance includes the money that was there at the start. The prototype confirms the intent (`selPointedTotal = opening + reconciled entries`). So the aggregate treats `is_system = 1` as reconciled, subject to the same `date <= statement_date` cut-off as everything else. The system entry stays untickable and its checkbox stays visually inert; nothing about `06`'s row behavior changes.

### Calculation

- `reconciled_balance` comes from the SQL aggregate above, **not** from summing a fetched entry list. The frontend holds a paged buffer that may cover 50 of 4,000 entries, so a frontend or use-case-level sum over "the entries" would silently mean "the entries paged in so far" — a wrong number that looks right. It would also load an unbounded row set to produce one integer.
- `delta` and the verdict are **pure functions in `domain::reconciliation`** over two `i64` cent values — `delta(bank_balance, reconciled_balance) = bank_balance - reconciled_balance`, and `is_balanced(delta) = delta == 0` — mirroring the existing pure `domain::entry::balance`. This is the rule the whole feature turns on; it is worth being verifiable with no database, no repository, and no fixtures.
- `usecases::reconciliation::reconciliation_summary(account_id)` glues the two: reads the stored settings, calls the aggregate when a statement date is set, applies the pure functions, and reads the unreconciled count. It returns **one** shape carrying: the statement date (optional), the bank balance (optional), the reconciled balance (optional — absent when no statement date is set), the delta (optional — absent when either input is), whether the account is balanced, and the unreconciled-entry count. One command, one round trip; the panel never assembles its own view from three calls that could disagree.
- Two write use cases, `set_bank_balance(account_id, amount)` and `set_statement_date(account_id, date)`, each returning the same freshly-recomputed summary shape, so a field edit and the figures it changes arrive together. `set_bank_balance` takes the raw major-unit `f64` exactly as typed and does the cents conversion via `money::to_cents` itself, per `technical-architecture.md` §1.3 and exactly as the opening-balance field already does; the summary's amounts are divided once at the command boundary on the way out.
- `ReconciliationError` as a `thiserror` enum, `#[derive(Serialize)]`, `#[serde(tag = "kind", content = "message")]` like `AccountError`/`EntryError`: unknown account, invalid amount, invalid date, and the I/O catch-all.
- `commands::reconciliation` exposes `reconciliation_summary`, `set_bank_balance`, and `set_statement_date`, mirroring the `Account`/`Entry` command module shape.

### The "unreconciled only" filter

- `unreconciled_only: bool` is added to `EntryListQuery` (Rust) and threaded through `list_entries` into `list_by_account`'s `WHERE` clause. **It is a backend query parameter, not a frontend filter over the paged buffer.** `EntriesPager` requests fixed-size pages; filtering client-side would let a page of 50 fully-reconciled entries render as an empty list still reporting `has_more: true`, and would make "how many rows are on screen" depend on how reconciled the account happens to be. The existing offset/limit/`has_more` contract only holds if the database applies every predicate.
- **The system entry is exempt, as it already is from the date-range filter.** `06-entries.md` made `list_by_account` always include it regardless of filters; that carries over unchanged. It is permanently unticked and would otherwise pass the filter anyway, but the exemption is stated so the two rules can't drift apart later.
- Jump-to-date (`offset_for_date`) applies the same predicate as `list_by_account`, for the same reason it already applies the date range: an offset computed under a different filter than the list points at the wrong row.

### Frontend

- New `ReconciliationApi` injectable service under `src/app/data/reconciliation`, wrapping `invoke()` for the three commands — the same seam `EntriesApi`/`AccountsApi` established and the one component tests mock. Components never call `invoke()` directly.
- The panel is its own presentational component under the `account` feature: inputs for the summary and the account's accent color, outputs for bank-balance change, statement-date change, and the filter checkbox toggle. It owns no fetching and no cross-component state — it renders what it is given and reports what the user did.
- **The panel-open / filter coupling lives in `Account`**, the component that already owns `sort`, `from`, `to`, and the `query()` builder those feed. Two signals — `panelOpen` and `includeReconciled` — and the effective predicate is `computed(() => panelOpen() && !includeReconciled())`, folded into `query()` alongside the existing filters (the sense is inverted deliberately: `includeReconciled` is what the "Inclure lignes pointées" checkbox shows, while the backend's `unreconciled_only` parameter and `EntriesPager`'s `unreconciledOnly` field keep their own, un-inverted meaning — only this one UI-facing signal and its negation in the computed changed). §4.3's "collapsing the panel disables the filter's effect regardless of the checkbox state" is then a property of one expression rather than a rule enforced by remembering to reset a flag in a close handler. Opening the panel leaves `includeReconciled` exactly as the user last set it (§4.3's "ticked by default when the panel is opened" — already-reconciled entries stay visible until the user unticks it); closing leaves the checkbox value alone too, since the computed already makes it inert. `panelOpen` starts `false` on every entry to the screen and is not persisted (§4.3's "collapsed by default").
- The checkbox is disabled when the summary's unreconciled count is zero.
- **Ticking a row while the filter is active refetches rather than patching in place.** `EntriesPager.setReconciled` currently patches the toggled row into the buffer deliberately, so the list doesn't jump under the pointer. That behavior is correct when the row still belongs in the list and is kept as the default. It is **wrong** when the filter is active, because the row no longer matches the active predicate: patching would leave a reconciled row sitting in a list that claims to show only unreconciled ones. So when the effective filter is on, a toggle reloads the page instead. Either way the summary is refetched, since the balance and delta changed. This is a deliberate, narrow exception to the in-place-patch pattern, justified by the predicate, not a reversal of it — the default path is untouched.
- Amounts and dates render through `DisplaySettingsService`'s existing `currency-format`/`date-format` pipes; no new formatting logic. The delta renders **signed** (per the §3.5 divergence noted above), colored `--pointage-ok` at exactly zero and `--pointage-bad` otherwise.
- The four `--pointage-*` theme tokens are added to the app's light and dark palettes with the prototype's values. Per business requirements §5, the panel's interactive elements (the "Pointage" toggle, the filter checkbox, the two inputs) take the current account's accent color like everything else on this screen; the red/green verdict colors are the one deliberate exception, since a semantic signal that changed color per account would signal nothing.
- Error display follows `technical-architecture.md` §2.2 as-is: a non-numeric bank balance is inline next to the field (it persists while the user corrects it), a rejected command is a toast.

## Testing Decisions

- **Domain unit tests** for `delta` and `is_balanced` over `i64` cents, with no repository: bank balance above, below, and exactly equal to the reconciled balance; both values negative; both zero. The exactly-equal case must assert `is_balanced` is true, since "green means exactly zero" is the feature's central claim.
- **Use-case tests** for `reconciliation_summary` against a hand-written in-memory fake `ReconciliationRepository` and the existing fake `EntryRepository` (no `mockall`, per `technical-architecture.md` §1.6): a fully-reconciled account reports balanced; an account with a discrepancy reports the signed delta; a `NULL` `statement_date` yields a summary with no reconciled balance and no delta rather than a zero or an error; a `NULL` `bank_balance` yields a reconciled balance but no delta; the unreconciled count is carried through.
- **Use-case tests** for `set_bank_balance` (a valid amount is converted to cents and stored; a sub-cent or non-numeric value is rejected as an invalid amount, matching the opening-balance field's rules) and `set_statement_date` (a valid date is stored; a malformed one is rejected), each asserting the returned summary reflects the value just written.
- **SQLite integration tests** for `sum_reconciled_up_to` against a fresh `:memory:` database with all migrations applied per test: only reconciled entries are counted; entries dated after the statement date are excluded even when reconciled; entries on the statement date exactly are included; the system entry is counted despite never being tickable; debits and credits are signed correctly; an account with no reconciled entries returns just the opening balance.
- **SQLite integration tests** for `count_unreconciled_by_account`: counts only non-system unreconciled entries, and returns zero for an account whose every real entry is reconciled (the case that grays the checkbox).
- **SQLite integration tests** for `unreconciled_only` on `list_by_account`: reconciled entries are excluded, the system entry is included regardless, and the flag composes correctly with an active date range and with both sort directions. Plus a pagination case proving a page is a page of _matching_ rows — an account where the first 50 entries are reconciled must still return a full page of unreconciled ones, not an empty page.
- **SQLite integration test** that `offset_for_date` applies the same predicate, so jump-to-date lands on the right row with the filter on.
- **Migration test** that `0007` applies to a database holding pre-existing accounts, leaving both new columns `NULL` on every one of them.
- **Angular component tests (Vitest)** for the panel component with a mocked summary input: renders reconciled balance, bank balance, statement date and delta; shows the green verdict and label at zero delta and the red one otherwise; renders the signed delta; shows the set-a-statement-date prompt instead of figures when the statement date is absent; disables the checkbox when the unreconciled count is zero; emits its three outputs on edit; shows an inline error for a non-numeric bank balance without emitting.
- **Angular component tests (Vitest)** for `Account`, mocking `ReconciliationApi` and `EntriesApi` (never `invoke()`): the panel is absent/collapsed on load; the "Pointage" toggle opens it and requests the summary; opening it leaves "unreconciled only" off and requests entries without the flag; ticking the checkbox re-requests entries with the flag set; unticking it re-requests without the flag; **collapsing the panel re-requests without the flag even when the checkbox stayed checked** (§4.3's rule, and the one most likely to regress); the checkbox state survives a close/reopen of the panel; toggling a row's checkbox with the filter active reloads the list and refetches the summary; toggling it with the filter inactive keeps the existing in-place patch and still refetches the summary; editing bank balance and statement date call the matching `ReconciliationApi` methods and update the displayed figures.
- **E2E (WebdriverIO, `e2e/reconciliation.e2e.ts`)**, per `technical-architecture.md` §2.3, sharing the session: open an account with entries, open the Pointage panel, confirm the list narrowed to unreconciled entries, set a statement date and a bank balance, tick entries until the delta reaches zero and assert the verdict flips to the balanced state, then collapse the panel and confirm the full list is back with the reconciled entries visible again — asserting on real `data-testid` hooks throughout.

## Out of Scope

- **Any reconciliation indicator on the home screen** (business requirements §4.1) — settled against in `03-accounts.md` and recorded in `dependencies.md`. Not deferred, not a gap: it is never built.
- Reconciliation figures on the account settings modal (§4.2). The two new fields are edited from the panel only; the modal's shape is unchanged.
- Multi-account or aggregate reconciliation. Every figure here is scoped to one account, per §3.5.
- Any history of past statements or reconciliation sessions: the two columns hold the current values and are overwritten, per §3.5's "no dedicated stored entity".
- Auto-matching entries against an imported statement, and statement import in any form — nowhere in the business requirements.
- Bulk "tick everything up to this date". Ticking stays one row at a time through `06-entries.md`'s existing checkbox.
- Recurring entries (§3.4) — `07-recurring-entries.md`. Generated entries are ordinary entries and reconcile like any other; nothing here anticipates them.
- Statistics (§4.5) — `09-statistics.md`.

## Further Notes

- The **system-entry-counts-as-reconciled** decision is the one place this spec resolves an inconsistency rather than implementing a requirement. `06-entries.md` made the system entry untickable; §3.5's formula counts only ticked entries; together they would put the opening balance permanently outside the reconciled balance and make green unreachable. The prototype's arithmetic settles it, but it is worth a second reader at review, because it is the single decision that would make every figure in the panel wrong if it went the other way.
- `dependencies.md` should be updated when this lands: `08-reconciliation` moves out of "layer 3 · not yet written".
- The three layer-3 specs are mutually independent, but this one and `07-recurring-entries.md` both extend `EntryRepository`. This spec only **adds** methods and one query-struct field, leaving `06`'s signatures alone, so the two should merge cleanly; whichever lands second should confirm that rather than assume it.
- `09-statistics.md` will want per-category and per-month aggregates over the same table. `sum_reconciled_up_to` is the second filtered-aggregate sibling of `sum_by_account`; if a third arrives there, that is the point to consider a general aggregate shape rather than a fourth bespoke method — not now, with two.
- `technical-architecture.md` §2.1 names "the reconciliation indicator's behavior" as its worked example of where `@angular/aria` earns its place over spartan. The verdict badge and the panel's disclosure behavior are that indicator; use it there.
